About the Pete Plan

This app applies the Pete Plan — a structured indoor rowing program by Pete Marston. Two modes: a 24-week Beginner build-up and a Standard 3-week continuous cycle.

Beginner vs Standard — which one?

The criterion is capability, not a fixed 2k time. A single 2k cutoff doesn't work because it varies by age, weight, and sex.

Choose Beginner if you're new to structured training, can't yet do 30 min continuous comfortably, have little interval experience, or can't complete Standard interval sessions at target pace. Move to Standard once you can handle those. Use the self-check in Plan Setup.

Standard Pete Plan (3-week cycle)

Fixed 7-day weekly layout — 3 hard sessions + 3 steady + 1 rest:

DaySession
Day 1Speed interval (rotates by week)
Day 2Steady distance (8–15 km, easy)
Day 3Endurance interval (rotates by week)
Day 4Steady distance
Day 5Hard distance (5k+)
Day 6Steady distance
Day 7Complete rest

Steady days exist to recover for the next hard session — ≥10s slower than your endurance interval pace, 22–25 spm. "If in doubt, go slower." The speed & endurance intervals rotate each week:

Speed intervalEndurance interval
Week 18 × 500m / 3:30R5 × 1500m / 5:00R
Week 2250-500-750-1k-750-500-250 / 1:30R per 250m4 × 2000m / 5:00R
Week 34 × 1000m / 5:00R3k, 2.5k, 2k / 5:00R

Pacing — every session is anchored to your 2k PB (offset = seconds per 500m vs your 2k split):

First time through, start at a pace you know you can hold — then beat that average next cycle. Targets are fixed by your 2k reference and only change when you update it. Sources: thepeteplan.wordpress.com + Concept2 Forum Pete Plan pacing consensus.

Beginner Pete Plan (24 weeks)

A 24-week progressive build-up. Each week has 3 core sessions plus 2 optional ones (shown in brackets). Volume rises ~500m/week early on, stabilises, then peaks before a 2k test. First time through, you can halve every session. Pacing is progressive (beat your last session). The full week-by-week schedule is shown live in the Dashboard.

Watts Formula

W = 2.80 / (pace_sec_per_meter)³

Official Concept2 conversion. This is why 1:35 → 1:30 on a 500m needs ~18% more power.
